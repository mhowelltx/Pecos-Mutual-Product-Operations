# Run the sprint review and retrospective

## When to use

On the afternoon of the last day of every sprint. Hold the sprint review first, then the retrospective.

## Roles

- **Product Owner:** leads the sprint review. You invite stakeholders and decide how the backlog changes.
- **Scrum Master:** facilitates both sessions, and leads the retrospective.
- **Developers:** demonstrate the work and take part fully in the retrospective.
- **Project Manager:** updates the milestone forecast at the review and raises any risk to the milestone plan.
- **Product Manager and stakeholders:** join the review to give feedback. They don't attend the retrospective.

## Before you start

- Check which items meet the Definition of Done. Only those are shown.
- Prepare a working demo in a production-like environment, not slides.
- Invite stakeholders at least a week ahead, using the shared sprint calendar.

## Steps: sprint review (1 hour)

1. **Restate the sprint goal** and say whether the team met it.
2. **Demonstrate the done work.** Developers show real software, in the order it serves the goal. Stakeholders try it where they can.
3. **Gather feedback.** Ask stakeholders what they would change and what they need next. The Product Owner notes each point.
4. **Update the forecast.** The Project Manager shows the milestone plan and says whether the next milestone is on track, at risk or late, and why.
5. **Adjust the backlog.** The Product Owner says which items move up, move down or are new, based on the feedback.

## Steps: retrospective (90 minutes)

1. **Set the stage.** Remind the team the retrospective is a safe space, and review the actions from the last retrospective.
2. **Gather data.** Each person notes what helped and what got in the way this sprint. Bring the facts: the impediment log, Run time spent and how the sprint goal went.
3. **Find patterns.** Group the notes and vote on the two or three topics that matter most.
4. **Dig into causes.** For the top topic, ask why until you reach something the team can change.
5. **Agree one or two actions.** Each action is small enough to try next sprint, with an owner. Add it to the sprint backlog for the next sprint.
6. **Close.** Check in quickly: was this retrospective a good use of time?

## Outputs

- Stakeholder feedback, and the backlog changes that follow from it.
- An updated milestone forecast: on track, at risk or late.
- One or two improvement actions with owners, added to the next sprint.

## Pitfalls

- **Slides instead of software.** Stakeholders learn the most by seeing and trying the real thing.
- **Showing work that isn't done.** It sets wrong expectations. Leave it on the backlog.
- **Too many actions.** Ten actions get none done. Pick one or two and follow through.
