The value delivery system is how Pecos Mutual turns a business need into a working product and then keeps it running. Every piece of work belongs to one of two workstreams, and each workstream has its own teams, cadence and playbooks.

## What it is

**Run** is run-the-business work. It keeps the platforms that policyholders, agents and employees use every day healthy and available. Teams do Run work through two practices:

- **Incident Management:** triage, declare and resolve incidents, then learn from them in a post-incident review.
- **Platform Health:** watch reliability, cost and risk for each platform, and review it every month.

**Change** is work that makes something new or better. It comes in two kinds:

- **Strategic projects** are larger investments the business chooses on purpose. A cross-functional project team delivers each one.
- **Discretionary** work is smaller, ongoing improvement. The static **Home Team** that owns the area delivers it from its own backlog.

## The Change lifecycle

Strategic projects move through three stages. Each stage ends with a clear decision, so nobody starts building before the business has agreed what to build.

1. **Discovery:** high-level ideation, estimation and prioritization. Business Executives work with Product Managers to shape ideas into one-page business cases, review a high-level design, size the work and rank it against everything else in the portfolio. Discovery ends at **Gate 2: Ready for planning**.
2. **Planning:** Resource Management and planning practices turn a prioritized project into goals and milestones the team can deliver within about 3 months. Planning runs on a fixed quarterly cycle and ends at the **Quarterly Planning** session, where Business Executives sign off the plan.
3. **Delivery:** the project team practices Scrum, in two-week sprints, to deliver the milestones.

Discretionary work skips most of this. A Home Team takes it through a light intake into its own backlog and delivers it with the same Scrum practices. See [Run, Change and Discretionary work](/operating-model/run-change-and-discretionary-work/) for how to tell which path an item takes.

## Why it matters

Naming the workstream and the stage answers the questions that slow teams down:

- **Who owns this?** Run work belongs to the team that owns the platform. Strategic projects belong to a project team. Discretionary work belongs to a Home Team.
- **What happens next?** Each stage has playbooks with named roles, inputs and outputs.
- **Where does capacity go?** Planning reserves capacity for Run work before it commits anything to Change, so incidents don't quietly eat a project's plan.

## How it connects

- The [five roles](/operating-model/roles-at-a-glance/) each show up at different points: Product Managers lead Discovery, Program and Project Managers lead Planning, and Scrum Masters and Product Owners lead Delivery.
