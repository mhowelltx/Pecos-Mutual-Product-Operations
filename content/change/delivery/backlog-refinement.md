## When to use

Hold a refinement session in the middle of every sprint, about day 5. Keep refining between sessions as questions come up.

## Roles

- **Product Owner:** leads the content. You bring the ordered backlog and answer questions about value and acceptance criteria.
- **Scrum Master:** facilitates and keeps the session to its timebox.
- **Developers:** ask questions, split items and size them.
- **Product Manager** (optional): joins when items need a decision on scope or value.

## Before you start

- Order the top of the backlog against the next milestone in the milestone plan.
- Draft acceptance criteria for the top items, and share them a day ahead.
- Bring any feedback from the last sprint review that changes priorities.

## Steps

1. **Check the goal.** Restate the next milestone and its "done when", so everyone refines towards the same target.
2. **Walk the top items in order.** For each one, the Product Owner explains the value and the acceptance criteria.
3. **Ask and answer.** Developers ask what they need to know to build and test the item. Note any question you can't answer in the room, with an owner and a due date.
4. **Split large items.** If an item won't fit in one sprint, split it into smaller items that each deliver value on their own. Split by user journey or business rule, not by technical layer.
5. **Size the items.** Use the team's usual method, such as story points. Re-size anything that has changed since it was last sized.
6. **Check against the Definition of Ready.** Mark the items that meet it. Note what's missing for those that don't.
7. **Check dependencies.** If an item needs work from another team, confirm the date with that team before you mark the item ready.
8. **Stop at two sprints.** Once roughly two sprints' worth of items are ready, stop. Refining further ahead wastes effort on items that may change.

## Outputs

- About two sprints' worth of ready items at the top of the backlog.
- Updated sizes, and items split where needed.
- A list of open questions, each with an owner and a date.

## Pitfalls

- **Designing in the session.** If a conversation needs a whiteboard and an hour, take it offline with two or three people.
- **The Product Owner refining alone.** Items the team hasn't seen aren't ready, however well written they are.
- **Splitting by layer.** "Build the API" and "Build the screen" each deliver nothing on their own. Split so each item can be demonstrated.
