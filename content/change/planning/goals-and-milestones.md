## When to use

Use this playbook for every strategic project in the coming quarter, once capacity planning has given it a team. A project that continues from last quarter gets a fresh milestone plan too.

## Roles

- **Project Manager:** leads. You write the milestone plan and own its risks and dependencies.
- **Product Owner:** makes sure each milestone delivers something of value, and turns the first milestone into backlog items.
- **Product Manager:** checks that the goals still match the outcome in the business case.
- **Program Manager:** checks dependencies with other projects and confirms capacity.

## Before you start

- Get the business case, the high-level design and the capacity plan for the project.
- Book two working sessions with the Product Owner and a senior engineer from the project team.
- Copy the [milestone plan template](/templates/milestone-plan/).

## Steps

1. **Write 2 to 4 goals.** Each goal is an outcome the business will notice, such as "Agents can bind a small-business policy online without calling us". Tie every goal to the business case.
2. **Cut each goal into milestones.** A milestone is a working, demonstrable step, not a phase like "design done". Aim for one milestone every 2 to 6 weeks.
3. **Write "done when" for each milestone.** Make it testable: "done when three pilot agents bind a policy in production".
4. **Place milestones on sprints.** The quarter has six sprints. Put each milestone at the end of a sprint, and leave the last sprint's capacity at least half free for slips.
5. **Check the fit.** Add up the team-sprints for the milestones and compare the total with the project's capacity. If it doesn't fit, cut or move a milestone. Don't stretch the team.
6. **List dependencies.** For each milestone, name anything it needs from another team or project, with the date you need it. The Program Manager confirms each one with the other side.
7. **List risks.** Note the top risks with an owner and a mitigation for each. Carry over open risks from the design review.
8. **Prepare the first milestone.** With the Product Owner, break the first milestone into backlog items ready for refinement in sprint 1.
9. **Share the plan** two working days before Quarterly Planning.

## Outputs

- A milestone plan: goals, milestones with "done when" and target sprint, dependencies and risks.
- Backlog items for the first milestone, ready for refinement.
- Dependency requests sent to the other teams.

## Pitfalls

- **Milestones that are activities.** "Testing complete" doesn't show value. "Claims staff process a real claim end to end" does.
- **Back-loading.** If most milestones land in sprint 6, nothing is learned until it's too late to change course.
- **Treating the plan as fixed.** The plan is a forecast. The Project Manager updates it after every sprint review.
